Lasagna

Layers of rich beef bolognese, pasta sheets and creamy homemade bechamel, baked until bubbling and golden, then finished with fresh basil.

It's built on our Beef Bolognese, so make a big batch of that first (or use leftovers) and the lasagna itself comes together quickly.

Don't skip the rest at the end. Ten to fifteen minutes out of the oven lets the layers set, so you get clean slices instead of a sloppy pile.

Pin it

Servings

6 servings

Quick Stats

1.5–2 hrs • Comfort Food • Base: 6 servings

Ingredients

  • 1 batch beef bolognese sauce
  • 1 pack lasagna sheets (about 250g)
  • 75g butter
  • 75g plain flour
  • 600ml milk
  • 150g grated cheese
  • 1 handful basil leaves

Method

  1. Make the meat sauce:
    Prepare the beef bolognese sauce and let it cool slightly so it is easier to layer.
  2. Make the bechamel:
    Melt the butter in a saucepan. Stir in the flour and cook for 1 minute, then gradually whisk in the milk. Cook until smooth and thickened, seasoning to taste.
  3. Layer the lasagna:
    Spread meat sauce over the base of a baking dish, add pasta sheets, then bechamel. Repeat the layers, finishing this stage with meat sauce.
  4. Bake covered:
    Cover with foil and bake at 180°C for 30 to 40 minutes, until the pasta is tender and the sauce is bubbling.
  5. Finish uncovered:
    Remove the foil. Add the final layer of bechamel and scatter over the cheese. Bake uncovered for another 15 to 20 minutes until golden.
  6. Rest and serve:
    Rest for 10 to 15 minutes, then top with basil leaves and slice.

Key Nutrients & Benefits

The beef bolognese brings plenty of protein, iron and zinc, while the milk and cheese in the bechamel add calcium. Slow-cooked tomatoes are a great source of lycopene. It's a rich, comforting dish, so serve it with a simple green salad.

Smart Swaps & Variations

  • Cheese blend: use a mix of mozzarella for stretch and Parmesan for flavour.
  • Add greens: layer in some wilted spinach or a ricotta and spinach mix.
  • Vegetarian: swap the bolognese for a lentil and mushroom ragù.
  • Fresh pasta sheets: they don't need pre-cooking and give a softer, silkier lasagna.
  • Make ahead: assemble a day ahead and bake when needed, or freeze individual portions once cooked.
