One Pot Creamy Paprika Beef Pasta

Smoky paprika beef mince, a rich tomato sauce, and rigatoni all cooked together in one pot — no separate boiling, no draining, just one pan to wash up.

A splash of double cream and a handful of wilted spinach at the end round it out into something that tastes like it took a lot more effort than it did.

Good for a weeknight when you want something warm and comforting without the cleanup.

Pin it

Servings

5 servings

Quick Stats

35–40 mins • High Protein • Base: 5 servings

Ingredients

  • 500g rigatoni, uncooked
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 brown onion, finely diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 500g lean beef mince (10% fat)
  • 1 tbsp smoked paprika
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper
  • 1.5 tbsp Italian herbs
  • 2 tsp chilli flakes (optional)
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 800g whole peeled tomatoes
  • 650ml low-sodium beef stock
  • 0.5 cup double cream
  • 2 large handfuls fresh baby spinach
  • Salt & pepper, to taste
  • Parmesan, to serve

Method

  1. Sauté the onion and garlic:
    On medium-high heat, add olive oil to your pan and sauté the onion until softened and fragrant, about 5 minutes. Add the garlic and sauté for a further 30 seconds.
  2. Brown the beef and add the spices:
    Add the beef mince and cook until browned, then add the smoked paprika, cayenne pepper, Italian herbs and chilli flakes.
  3. Cook out the tomato paste:
    Add the tomato paste and stir through until combined. Cook for about 2 minutes.
  4. Add the tomatoes, liquid and pasta:
    Add the whole peeled tomatoes and break them up in the pan. Cook for a further 5 minutes, then add the stock, cream and rigatoni. Stir through until the rigatoni is lightly covered by the liquid — top up with a splash of extra stock or water if it looks short.
  5. Simmer with the lid on:
    Turn the heat down to medium-low, place the lid on, and cook for 15 minutes or until the pasta is cooked through.
  6. Wilt the spinach:
    Remove the lid and stir through the spinach until wilted, 1-2 minutes.
  7. Finish and serve:
    Stir through the parmesan and serve.

Key Nutrients & Benefits

Lean beef mince brings a solid hit of protein and iron, tomatoes add lycopene and vitamin C, and spinach folds in extra iron and fibre without changing the flavour.

Smart Swaps & Variations

  • Lighter version — swap the double cream for light cream or a splash of milk.
  • Chicken or turkey mince instead of beef — leaner option, same method.
  • Extra veg — diced capsicum or zucchini can go in with the onion.
  • Less heat — skip the cayenne pepper and chilli flakes if you want it mild.
  • Gluten-free — use a gluten-free rigatoni; cooking time may vary slightly.
